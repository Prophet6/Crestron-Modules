Start here

Date v1.0 reads the processor clock and writes Full_Date. Four dropdowns spell the day of week, the day of the month, the month, and the year. Each includes Off. Final_Format is the date shape. Week_Format is where Week n sits, or Off. Neither dropdown replaces the spellings.

Drop the symbol

It lives in Time/Date. The defaults already publish a full US date: Monday, January 3rd, 2026 on a Monday the 3rd.

Pulse it at midnight

Tie Refresh to a When at 00:00. The module also publishes once at startup, and again whenever a latched override changes.

F1 is this page

Leave Date v1.0.html next to Date v1.0.usp. The comment inside the module is the short version.

  1. Add Date v1.0 from Time/Date.
  2. Leave the spelling dropdowns, or set any of them to Off.
  3. Pick a date shape. The name is a picture of that punctuation, not today's date.
  4. Leave Week_Format Off, or pick where the week number sits.
  5. Join Full_Date to the touchpanel serial.
  6. Pulse Refresh from a When at midnight so the string rolls over.
Press F1 on Date v1.0 in SIMPL Windows to open this page. The spelling and date-shape names use Monday, the 3rd, January, and 2026 so you can see the shape. Week Format uses <date> for the date those dropdowns build. The processor clock supplies the real day.

Refresh

The module does not watch the clock. Three things rewrite Full_Date:

  • Program start, after the other symbols have initialized.
  • A rising edge on Refresh.
  • A change on an override that has already latched.

A When symbol at midnight, driving Refresh, is the usual rollover. Pulse it any other time you want the string caught up, for example after the processor clock is set.

Overrides

Each dropdown has an analog override on the same row. The numbers are the same numbers as the dropdown: 0 is the first entry, then 1, 2, and so on. An Analog Initialize can send the value you would have picked in SIMPL Windows.

Until a value arrives on that input after startup, the dropdown is in charge. The 0 that an unwired analog sits at does not count. After the first real change, that analog stays in charge. Sending 0 later is a real choice: Off for a piece, or the first order for Final_Format. It does not fall back to the dropdown.

A value past the last entry is clamped to the last entry. It does not clear the string.

Do not treat override 0 as "use the parameter." That is true only before the input has ever changed. After it latches, 0 means Off, or the order Monday, January 3rd, 2026.

Week

The week text is always Week plus the number, with no leading zero: Week 1, Week 53. Week_Format does not change that spelling. It chooses where the week sits relative to the date, or Off. The live number follows the calendar. See Week number and Week placement.

Date shape

Final_Format is the punctuation of the date. Word shapes are pictured with Monday, 3rd, January, and 2026. Numeric shapes are pictured with 1, 3, and 2026. Change a spelling and the same shape changes with it. Turn a piece Off and that piece, plus the comma or space that belonged to it, drops out.

Monday, January 3rd is the first shape with the year Off. January 3rd, 2026 is that shape with the day of week Off. January 2026 is Monday, 3rd January 2026 with the day of week and the day Off. Those are not separate rows.

There is no Off for the date shape. Value 0 is Monday, January 3rd, 2026.

ValueNameSlots
0Monday, January 3rd, 2026Day of week, month and day, year. Comma before the year.
1Monday, January 3rd 2026Same slots, with a space before the year.
2Monday, 3rd January 2026Day of week, then day, month, and year. Day of week and day Off leaves January 2026.
3Monday, 1/3/2026Day of week, then month, day, and year with slashes.
4Monday, 3/1/2026Day of week, then day, month, and year with slashes.
5Monday, 2026-01-03Day of week, then year, month, and day with hyphens.

The numeric names show the usual pairings. The shape still uses the spelling dropdowns, so the defaults on Monday, 1/3/2026 read Monday, January/3rd/2026. Set the month to 1 or 01, the day to 3 or 03, and the year to 26 or 2026 when you want digits.

On Monday, 3rd January 2026, choosing the word Third inserts "of" and a comma before the year: Monday, Third of January, 2026. With the day of week Off that is Third of January, 2026. With the day of week and the day both Off, the comma goes with them and the string is January 2026.

On Monday, 1/3/2026, Monday, 3/1/2026, and Monday, 2026-01-03, the comma belongs to the day of week. Turn that spelling Off and the string is 1/3/2026, 3/1/2026, or 2026-01-03.

Week placement

Week_Format default is Off. The other choices attach Week n to whatever date shape you picked. <date> in the name is that date. January 2026 with the week in front is Week 1, January 2026.

ValueNameWhere the week goes
0OffNo week number
1Week 1, <date>In front, with a comma
2<date>, Week 1After the date, with a comma
3<date> (Week 1)After the date, in parentheses
4Week 1: <date>In front, with a colon

Parentheses and the colon appear only when the week and the date are both present. Week alone is Week 1. Turn the date pieces Off when the string should be the week by itself.

A single field is a shape that contains it, with the other pieces Off. Monday alone is the default shape with the day, month, and year Off. 2026 alone is that shape with the day of week, day, and month Off. The default shape keeps a comma: with the day Off it reads January, 2026.

Week number

The week is the US calendar week. It starts on Sunday, the same Sunday as GetDayOfWeekNum (0 is Sunday, 6 is Saturday). Week 1 is the week that contains January 1. The number runs from 1 to 53. This is not the ISO week, which starts on Monday.

DateWeekdayWeek
January 1, 2026Thursday1
January 3, 2026Saturday1
January 4, 2026Sunday2
December 31, 2026Thursday53
February 29, 2024Thursday9
December 31, 2024Tuesday53

February 29 is included in a leap year. A leap year divides by 4, skips years that divide by 100, and still includes years that divide by 400.

Empty string

Full_Date is empty only when every piece is Off and the week is Off. Every shape includes the day of week. Turn that spelling Off and the comma that belonged to it drops out.
